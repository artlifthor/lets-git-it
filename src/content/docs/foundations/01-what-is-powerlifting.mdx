## In one minute

Powerlifting is a strength sport with three lifts: the **squat**, the **bench press**, and the **deadlift**. A lifter gets three attempts at each, and the best successful attempt on each lift adds up to a **total**, which decides placings within weight class and age division. Competitions are either **classic (raw)**, with minimal gear, or **equipped**, with supportive suits and shirts. Worldwide, drug-tested powerlifting is governed by the **International Powerlifting Federation (IPF)**; in the Philippines, its national member is the **Powerlifting Association of the Philippines (PAP)**. Coaches program toward this test, so knowing the rules shapes every training decision.

## Prerequisites

None. This is the first chapter.

## The three lifts

Lifts are contested in this order at every meet. A lift counts only if a majority of the three referees give a white light.

| Lift | What the referees look for (summary) | Key commands |
|---|---|---|
| **Squat** | The top of the hip crease must drop below the top of the knee; then stand fully upright with knees locked | "Squat" to start, "Rack" to finish |
| **Bench press** | Lower the bar to the chest or abdomen, hold it motionless, press to full lockout; head, shoulders and buttocks stay on the bench, feet stay flat on the floor, and the thumbs wrap around the bar | "Start", "Press", "Rack" |
| **Deadlift** | Lift the bar in one motion to a fully upright stance with knees locked and shoulders back; no hitching (resting the bar on the thighs) | "Down" to finish |

This is a summary only. The full and current requirements are in the **IPF Technical Rules**, which every competing lifter and coach should read.

## How a meet works

- **Weigh-in** happens before competition, usually two hours before the start, and decides the lifter's weight class.
- Each lifter takes **three attempts** on each lift. After a good lift, the next attempt must go up by at least 2.5 kg; a missed attempt can be repeated at the same weight.
- The **best successful attempt** on each lift is added to make the **total**. A lifter who misses all three attempts on any lift has no total.
- Lifters are ranked by total within their **weight class** and **age division**. Best-lifter awards compare lifters across weight classes using a points formula (see *Scoring* below).

:::tip[In plain words]
A meet is nine tries at lifting as heavy as you can: three squats, three bench presses, three deadlifts. Your best good lift of each kind is added up. Whoever has the biggest total in your bodyweight group and age group wins.
:::

## Classic (raw) vs. equipped

| | **Classic (raw)** | **Equipped** |
|---|---|---|
| Allowed gear | Singlet, belt, wrist wraps, knee sleeves, shoes, socks, T-shirt | Everything in classic, plus supportive squat/deadlift suits, bench shirts, and knee wraps |
| What it tests | Strength of the lifter with minimal support | Strength plus skill in using the supportive equipment |
| Popularity | Most lifters today, including most new lifters | Smaller, specialised group |

All gear worn on the platform must be on the IPF's approved list. This manual focuses on classic lifting unless a chapter says otherwise.

## Federations

- The **IPF** is the international governing body for drug-tested powerlifting, with regional federations below it. In Asia, that is the **Asian Powerlifting Federation (APF)**.
- Each country has one national IPF member federation. Its national records and selections for international meets run through that federation.
- Many other federations exist outside the IPF, with their own rules. Some do not drug test. Records and results from different federations are not directly comparable.

## Weight classes

IPF weight classes, used by PAP for its main divisions:

| | Weight classes (kg) |
|---|---|
| **Men** | 59 · 66 · 74 · 83 · 93 · 105 · 120 · 120+ |
| **Women** | 47 · 52 · 57 · 63 · 69 · 76 · 84 · 84+ |
| **Extra class for younger lifters** | Men 53 · Women 43 (sub-junior and junior age groups) |

A lifter in the 83 kg class must weigh **83.00 kg or less** at weigh-in (and more than 74.00 kg).

## Age divisions

PAP's records list these divisions. The IPF divisions are sub-junior, junior, open and masters; PAP adds national divisions for younger and newer lifters.

| Division | Age (from PAP's records) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-Junior** | 14–18 | IPF division |
| **Junior** | 19–23 | IPF division |
| **Open** | No age limit | IPF division; anyone eligible may enter |
| **Master I / II / III / IV** | 40+ / 50+ / 60+ / 70+ | IPF divisions |
| **Developmental, High School, College, Novice** | Set by PAP | PAP national divisions; check PAP for current eligibility |

## Scoring across weight classes

Heavier lifters lift more in absolute terms, so a points formula is used to compare lifters of different bodyweights, for example for "best lifter" awards.

- The **IPF uses IPF GL Points** (adopted in 2020, replacing earlier formulas such as Wilks).
- Many other federations and ranking sites use **DOTS**.
- Both convert a total and a bodyweight into one score. They give similar but not identical rankings, so always state which formula you are using.

:::tip[In plain words]
A 100 kg lifter will usually lift more than a 60 kg lifter, so comparing their totals isn't fair. Points formulas give each lifter a score that adjusts for body weight. The IPF and PAP use one called IPF GL points; other groups often use one called DOTS.
:::

## Drug testing

IPF powerlifting is drug tested, and the IPF follows the World Anti-Doping Code. Lifters can be tested at competitions and out of competition. A positive test brings a ban and loss of results; under PAP's record rules, the lifter also forfeits all records set from 1 January 2011 up to the date of the positive test. Coaches should check every medication and supplement a lifter takes against the current prohibited list before competing.

## Powerlifting in the Philippines

**The national federation.** PAP describes itself as:

- the official **national federation member of the IPF and the Asian Powerlifting Federation**,
- the **National Sports Association (NSA)** for powerlifting recognised by the **Philippine Sports Commission (PSC)**, and
- an **associate member of the Philippine Olympic Committee (POC)**.

PAP is listed among the IPF's Asian member federations.

**Competitions.** Meet names that appear in PAP's official national records include:

- **Raw Nationals** (also run as SBD Raw Nationals), the main national championship for classic lifting
- **Equipped Nationals**
- **Regional meets**, such as the Luzon Classic, the Visayas Classic and Visayas Powerlifting Championships, and DPL regional legs

Filipino lifters also set national records at international IPF-affiliated meets, such as the Asian Classic Powerlifting Championships, the World Junior and Sub-Junior Championships, and the Southeast Asian Cup.

**Getting started in the Philippines.** For a lifter's first meet:

1. Train with or join a **PAP-registered team or gym**. Only PAP-registered team names appear in PAP's records.
2. Register as a PAP lifter and enter a meet through **PAP's official channels**; entry steps and fees change, so always check the current announcement.
3. Start at a **regional meet** or in the **Novice** division before targeting Nationals.
4. Read the current IPF Technical Rules, and check every supplement and medication against the anti-doping rules.

**National records** for every division are on the [PAP National Records](/lets-git-it/pap-records/) page, updated monthly from PAP's official records.

## Common mistakes

- **Training without knowing the rules.** Squats that are high, benches without a pause, or ignoring commands cost lifters at meets; train to competition standard from early on.
- **Choosing a weight class too late.** Decide well before a meet whether the lifter will compete in their current class; making weight is covered in Part 4.
- **Comparing results across federations.** Totals from untested or non-IPF meets are not the same test.
- **Not checking supplements.** A positive test from a contaminated product still counts.
- **Relying on old information.** Rules, equipment lists and meet entry steps change; use the current IPF rulebook and PAP announcements.

## Key takeaways

- Powerlifting tests the squat, bench press and deadlift: three attempts each, best of each added into a total.
- Classic (raw) allows minimal gear; equipped allows supportive suits and shirts.
- The IPF governs drug-tested powerlifting worldwide; PAP is its national member in the Philippines.
- Weight class and age division decide who a lifter competes against; IPF GL points compare lifters across weight classes.
- Coaches should program for the competition standard from the start.
