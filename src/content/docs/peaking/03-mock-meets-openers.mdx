## In one minute

A **mock meet** rehearses competition in training: the lifts in meet order, with commands, warm-ups and attempt timing, so the lifter, coach and handler practise the day before it counts. An **opener test** is smaller: one single at the planned first attempt, with commands, about 7–14 days before the meet. The opener should be a weight the lifter can make on a bad day, often described as a weight they could triple when fresh; in competition data, lifters opened at about 91–92% of their best successful lift on the day. Mock meets are for **rehearsal and information**, not for testing true maxes: the closer to the meet, the more an all-out max costs and the less it tells you. Use the results to set openers, plan second and third attempts, and fix anything that would cost a red light.

## Mock meets, opener tests and heavy singles

| Session | What happens | When | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full mock meet** | Squat, bench press, deadlift in order; full warm-ups; 2–3 attempts each; commands; meet timing | About 3–5 weeks out | Rehearse the whole day; data for attempts |
| **Opener test** | One single per lift at the planned opener, with commands; usually on separate days | About 7–14 days out | Confirm openers; confidence |
| **Heavy single** | A single at RPE 8–9 in a normal session | 2–4 weeks out | Estimate current strength |
| **Commands practice** | Light singles with a coach calling commands | Any time; especially meet week | Habit; no red lights |

<Evidence level="practice" /> There is no research comparing these approaches or on mock meets themselves. What follows is coaching practice, supported where possible by survey and competition data.

:::tip[In plain words]
A mock meet is a dress rehearsal for competition day. An opener test is a quick check that your first attempts feel easy. Neither is a test of your best lifts; you save those for the meet.
:::

## Why rehearse

- **Skill under meet conditions.** <Evidence level="practice" /> Waiting for "Squat", pausing to "Press", holding the lockout for "Rack" and "Down", and getting depth right are skills. They fail under pressure if they were never practised (rules overview in [Part 1 · Chapter 1](/lets-git-it/foundations/01-what-is-powerlifting/)).
- **A routine that works.** <Evidence level="practice" /> Warm-up timing, food and caffeine, belt and wraps, chalk, how long to wait between attempts: the mock meet shows what works before it matters.
- **Reliable information.** <Evidence level="moderate" /> A systematic review of 32 studies found 1RM testing has good to excellent test–retest reliability, with a median coefficient of variation of about 4% (Grgic et al., 2020). A well-run heavy session gives useful numbers, but a difference of a few percent between two days can be normal variation. Don't rebuild a plan around one slightly bad day.
- **Confidence.** <Evidence level="practice" /> Making an opener easily, with commands, 1–2 weeks out is one of the best ways to calm a nervous lifter.

:::tip[In plain words]
Practise the meet so nothing surprises you on the day: the commands, the timing, the warm-ups, the food. Heavy lifts in training tell you roughly how strong you are, but one bad day isn't a reason to panic.
:::

## When to run them

<Evidence level="practice" /> **Full mock meet: about 3–5 weeks out.** Close enough that the numbers are relevant; far enough out that the lifter can recover and still complete the heaviest part of the peak and the taper. A mock meet with heavy second attempts is the heaviest session of the week and replaces the normal heavy singles.

<Evidence level="practice" /> **Opener tests: about 7–14 days out.** North American raw lifters reported their final heavy squat and deadlift sessions **7–10 days** out and their final heavy bench press **less than 7 days** out, with final heavy lifts at about **90–92.5% 1RM** (Travis et al., 2021b). An opener test fits that timing and load exactly. A systematic review of survey studies found the same lift order: deadlifts last done 6–8 days out, squats 5–6.5 days, bench press 3–5 days (Winwood et al., 2026).

**Typical schedule (meet on Saturday, week 0):**

| Weeks out | Session | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 5–3 | Optional full mock meet (Saturday) | Only for lifters who need it (see below) |
| 3 | Heaviest singles (RPE 8.5–9) if no mock meet | [Chapter 2](/lets-git-it/peaking/02-building-a-peaking-block/) |
| 2 | Opener tests: squat ~12 days, deadlift ~9–10 days, bench ~7–8 days out | Full commands |
| 1 | Light singles and commands only | Nothing heavy |

**Who benefits most from a full mock meet?** Novices before a first meet, lifters who have red-lighted for commands or depth, lifters with a new handler or coach, and lifters changing something (new weight class, new gear, new technique). A lifter with many meets and a stable routine may need only opener tests.

:::tip[In plain words]
If you do a full practice meet, do it about a month before competition. Test your openers one to two weeks before, deadlift first and bench press last. The last week is for light lifts only.
:::

## How to run a mock meet

**1. Set it up like the meet.**

- Same order (squat, bench press, deadlift), same gear, same start time if possible, same food and caffeine plan.
- A coach or training partner calls the **commands**: squat "Squat" … "Rack"; bench press "Start" … "Press" … "Rack"; deadlift "Down". Hold each command until the lift is clearly finished and still.
- Film from the side for depth (squat) and from the front for lockouts and bench pause.
- Judge strictly. A squat that was "probably deep" is a red light.

**2. Use meet timing.** <Evidence level="practice" /> Under the IPF Technical Rules, a lifter has **one minute** to start an attempt once the bar is announced as ready, and the next attempt must be handed in within **one minute** of the referees' decision on the previous one. Check the current IPF Technical Rules, as details can change (International Powerlifting Federation; see [Chapter 7: Competition rules](/lets-git-it/peaking/07-competition-rules/)). In a mock meet, practise choosing the next attempt within that minute. Rest between attempts as in a typical flight, about **6–10 minutes**, and leave **20–40 minutes** between lifts.

**3. Rehearse the warm-ups.** <Evidence level="practice" /> Plan the warm-up backward from the opener: 5–7 warm-up sets, the last at about 90–95% of the opener, finished about 6–10 minutes before the opener ([Part 1 · Chapter 9](/lets-git-it/foundations/09-session-structure-warm-ups/)). Note how long the warm-ups actually took; that number goes into the meet-day plan ([Chapter 6: Meet day](/lets-git-it/peaking/06-meet-day/)).

**4. Cap effort.** <Evidence level="practice" /> Openers at about RPE 7–8, second attempts at about RPE 8.5–9, and third attempts only if the second was RPE 8 or easier, still capped at RPE 9. **No grinders and no misses on purpose.** In 15 powerlifters, even a true 1RM was rated only about 9.6–9.7 on average (Helms et al., 2017), so an RPE 9 single is already close to a max and gives almost the same information.

**5. Record everything.** Load, RPE, bar speed or video, command errors, warm-up times, how the lifter felt, food and caffeine timing.

:::tip[In plain words]
Run your practice meet like the real thing: same order, same gear, someone calling commands, strict judging, and the same rest times. Lift your openers and second attempts, but don't grind out a max. Write down what happened so you can plan the real day.
:::

## Choosing openers

The opener has one job: **get a total on the board.** A lifter who misses all three attempts on one lift is out of the competition (no total), however strong they are.

**What competition data show:**

- <Evidence level="emerging" /> At IPF Classic World Championships (2012–2019), 66 men and 43 women who made all their lifts opened at about **91% of their third attempt**, then added about **5%** for the second and about **3%** for the third (Travis et al., 2021). Men opened relatively heavier than women on the bench press and deadlift; women used bigger jumps between attempts on the bench press.
- <Evidence level="emerging" /> In 10,672 entries at raw Powerlifting Australia meets (2008–2019), lifters opened the squat at an average of **92% of their best successful lift** on the day, and 93.5% went on to improve on the opener. Winners opened their squat about 7 kg heavier than non-winners and made their third attempt slightly more often (about 68% vs 64%) (Howells et al., 2022). These are associations: stronger lifters open heavier and win more; it does not show that opening heavier makes you win.

<Evidence level="practice" /> **Coaching rules for the opener:**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| A weight the lifter could **triple** on a normal day (about 90–92% of expected best) | Makeable on a bad day, after a weigh-in, with nerves |
| About **RPE 7–8** as a single in training | Leaves margin for a slow command or a poor night's sleep |
| **Lighter** than the heaviest training single of the peak | Never open with a weight that was hard in training |
| **Technically perfect** under strict commands | Openers are lost to rules (depth, pause, early rack) more than to strength |
| **More conservative** for novices, after a big weight cut, after an injury, or when a bomb-out would be costly | Getting a total matters most |
| **Slightly heavier** is acceptable for experienced lifters with consistent openers | A higher opener can lift the total if it is truly safe |

**A simple calculation.** Estimate the lifter's current 1RM from the heaviest single of the peak using the RTS chart ([Part 1 · Chapter 5](/lets-git-it/foundations/05-measuring-intensity/)), then open at about 89–91% of it. Example: squat 172.5 kg at RPE 8.5 (93.9%) → estimated 1RM about 183.5 kg → opener 162.5–167.5 kg. Pick the lower end if in doubt.

**Second and third attempts** follow the opener: a second at about the best heavy single of the peak (around RPE 8.5–9 in training), and a third at or slightly above the estimated max, chosen on the day. Full attempt strategy is in [Chapter 5: Attempt selection strategy](/lets-git-it/peaking/05-attempt-selection/).

:::tip[In plain words]
Your first attempt should be boring: a weight you could lift three times on a normal day. Lifters at the world championships open at about 91% of their best lift. Missing all three attempts on one lift ends your meet, so pick an opener you will make even on a bad day.
:::

## What to do with the results

<Evidence level="practice" /> Use the opener test (or the mock-meet opener) to confirm or adjust:

| Opener test result | What it means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| RPE ≤ 7, fast, technically clean | Lifter is at or above the expected strength | Keep the opener (most coaches) or add 2.5 kg; raise planned 2nd/3rd attempts |
| RPE 7.5–8, solid | On plan | Keep the opener and attempt plan |
| RPE 8.5–9, slow | Fatigue, a bad day, or the opener is too heavy | Lower the opener 2.5–5 kg; review sleep, stress, weight cut; don't retest |
| Missed or near-miss | Opener too heavy, or a technical/command problem | Lower the opener at least 5%; fix the cause; a light commands session, not a retest |
| Red light for depth, pause or command | A rules problem, not strength | Keep the load if it moved well; practise commands at 70–85% for the rest of the peak |
| Lifter anxious or shaken | Confidence is low | Lower the opener 2.5 kg; a guaranteed opener is worth more than 2.5 kg |

**Do not retest a bad opener in the same week.** <Evidence level="practice" /> A second heavy test adds fatigue close to the meet and rarely changes the decision. Lower the opener and move on.

**After a full mock meet** (3–5 weeks out), also review: warm-up timing, attempt jumps, food and caffeine, gear, and command errors. Adjust the peak's remaining heavy singles to the new estimate of current strength.

:::tip[In plain words]
If your opener felt easy, keep it. If it felt hard, make it a bit lighter. If you missed it or got a red light, make it lighter and fix the problem, but don't test it again that week. Being sure of your first lift matters more than a few extra kilos.
:::

## When not to max before a meet

<Evidence level="practice" /> Avoid true maxes (RPE 10, grinding, or attempting a PR) in training:

- **In the last 2–3 weeks.** A max costs recovery exactly when fatigue should be falling, and the risk of a training injury is not worth a number you will lift on the platform anyway.
- **In meet week.** Nothing heavy; light singles and commands only.
- **When the lifter needs confidence.** A missed max 2 weeks out can do more harm than any information it gives. Heavy singles at RPE 8.5–9 give almost the same estimate.
- **For novices.** Their strength is rising quickly, and a meet is their max test.
- **After illness, injury, or a hard weight cut.** Lower expectations instead of testing.
- **When the lifter tends to "leave it in the gym".** Some lifters hit training PRs and then underperform at the meet; keep their training singles at RPE 8–8.5.

**If you need a max** (for example to qualify, or to set openers for a lifter with no recent heavy data), do it at least **4–5 weeks out**, then complete a normal peak afterwards.

:::tip[In plain words]
Don't try to lift your best weight in the last two or three weeks before a meet. You will be more tired on the day, and it won't tell you much that a heavy-but-not-maximal single doesn't. Save your best for the platform.
:::

## Sample programs

RPE loads come from the RTS chart ([Part 1 · Chapter 5](/lets-git-it/foundations/05-measuring-intensity/)), rounded to 2.5 kg. Meets are on a Saturday.

### Program 1: Full mock meet three weeks out (intermediate man)

**Lifter:** ~83 kg man; squat 180 kg, bench press 120 kg, deadlift 210 kg, following the 5-week peak in [Chapter 2](/lets-git-it/peaking/02-building-a-peaking-block/), Program 1. The mock meet is on the **Saturday of week 2 (21 days out)**. It replaces week 2's Thursday deadlift and Saturday sessions and week 3's heavy singles. Planned meet openers: 165 / 110 / 192.5 kg.

**The week of the mock meet (week 2)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8 (~92%, 165 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 157.5 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~92%, 110 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Tue | Bench press | 3 × 4 | RPE 7 (~81%, 97.5 kg) |
| Thu | Bench press, light | 3 × 2 | ~80% (95 kg), with commands |
| Thu | Squat, light | 2 × 2 | ~75% (135 kg), with commands |
| Fri | Rest | — | — |
| Sat | **Mock meet** | See below | — |

No deadlift this week before the mock: the last heavy pull was 9 days earlier (week 1, Thursday).

**Mock meet: warm-ups and attempts**

| Lift | Warm-ups (finish about 6–10 min before the opener) | 1st | 2nd | 3rd (only if 2nd was RPE ≤ 8; cap RPE 9) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Squat | Bar × 10, 60 × 5, 100 × 3, 125 × 2, 142.5 × 1, 155 × 1 | 165 (target RPE 7–7.5) | 172.5 (target RPE 8.5–9) | 177.5 |
| Bench press | Bar × 10, 50 × 5, 67.5 × 3, 82.5 × 2, 95 × 1, 102.5 × 1 (paused, commands) | 110 | 115 | 117.5 |
| Deadlift | 70 × 5, 120 × 3, 145 × 2, 167.5 × 1, 180 × 1 | 192.5 | 200 | 205 |

Rest 6–10 minutes between attempts and 20–40 minutes between lifts. The coach calls every command and submits the next attempt within one minute.

**The week after (week 3, replaces Chapter 2's heaviest singles)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat, light | 3 × 2 | ~80% (145 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 3 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Tue | Bench press | 3 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Tue | Chest-supported row | 2 × 10 | RPE 7 |
| Thu | Deadlift, light | 2 × 2 | ~80% (167.5 kg) |
| Thu | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8 (~92%, 110 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |
| Sat | Squat | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8 (~92%, 165 kg), then RPE 7 (~86%, 155 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | 2 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |

**Weeks 4–5:** as in [Chapter 2](/lets-git-it/peaking/02-building-a-peaking-block/), Program 1 (opener tests 12, 9 and 7 days out; light meet week), with openers adjusted from the mock-meet results using the table above.

**Why it's built this way:** the mock meet sits 3 weeks out, so there is a full week of easier training after it before the opener tests. Deadlift is kept light the week after, because the mock-meet pull was the heaviest of the peak. Second attempts at RPE 8.5–9 give the strength estimate; thirds are optional and never grinders.

### Program 2: Opener-test week with warm-up ladders (female lifter, 63 kg class)

**Lifter:** squat 120 kg, bench press 65 kg, deadlift 150 kg, following the 4-week peak in [Chapter 2](/lets-git-it/peaking/02-building-a-peaking-block/), Program 2. Planned openers 110 / 60 / 137.5 kg. No full mock meet; each opener is tested once, with commands, at the start of a normal session.

| Day | Lift | Warm-ups | Opener test | Rest of session |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon (12 days out) | Squat | Bar × 8, 40 × 5, 65 × 3, 80 × 2, 95 × 1, 102.5 × 1 | 110 × 1 with "Squat" and "Rack" | Squat 2 × 2 @ RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg); bench 2 × 2 @ RPE 7 (~86%, 55 kg) |
| Wed (10 days out) | Deadlift | 60 × 5, 85 × 3, 105 × 2, 120 × 1, 127.5 × 1 | 137.5 × 1 with "Down" | Bench 3 × 3 at ~73% (47.5 kg), paused |
| Fri (8 days out) | Bench press | Bar × 8, 30 × 5, 40 × 3, 47.5 × 2, 52.5 × 1, 55 × 1 (paused, commands) | 60 × 1 with "Start", "Press", "Rack" | Bench 1 × 2 @ RPE 7 (~86%, 55 kg); squat 2 × 2 at ~80% (95 kg) |

**Decision rules for each test:** use the results table above. For her bench press, a 2.5 kg change is about 4% of her max, so lower the bench opener by 2.5 kg only if the test was RPE 8.5 or harder.

**Why it's built this way:** testing each lift on a different day keeps each session short and focused, places the heavy deadlift 2 days after the heavy squat and 2 days before the light squat, and follows the reported order (deadlift last heavy earlier than bench press). Warm-ups match the plan she will use at the meet, so she also learns how long they take.

### Program 3: First meet for a novice (opener day 10 days out)

**Lifter:** novice man finishing linear progression; recent work sets: squat 3 × 5 at 85 kg, bench press 3 × 5 at 50 kg, deadlift 1 × 5 at 105 kg (estimated 1RMs about 100 / 60 / 120 kg). He has never competed. Training days: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

**Openers** are chosen from what he has done for clean sets, not from estimated maxes: about the weight of his recent sets of 3–5, which he could triple. Planned openers: **87.5 / 50 / 105 kg**.

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon (12 days out) | Normal session: squat 3 × 3 at 87.5 kg, bench 3 × 3 at 52.5 kg, with commands on every rep |
| **Wed (10 days out)** | **Mini mock meet:** squat, bench, deadlift in order; warm up to each opener; one single at the opener with full commands and strict judging; one second single at about +5% (92.5 / 52.5 / 110 kg) only if the opener was easy. No thirds. |
| Fri (8 days out) | Squat 2 × 3 at 80 kg; bench 3 × 3 at 47.5 kg; deadlift 1 × 3 at 95 kg |
| Mon (5 days out) | Squat 2 × 2 at 75 kg; bench 3 × 2 at 45 kg; commands practice |
| Wed (3 days out) | Squat 2 × 1 at 75 kg, bench 2 × 1 at 45 kg, with commands; last session |
| Sat | **Meet:** openers 87.5 / 50 / 105 kg; seconds around the mini-mock seconds; thirds a small PR only if the second was easy |

**Why it's built this way:** a first meet is about learning the commands, timing and routine, and getting a total. Openers he has lifted for multiple clean reps make a bomb-out very unlikely. The mini mock meet teaches the order of the day and the commands, without a max test he does not need.

## Common mistakes

- **Turning the mock meet into a max test.** Grinding PRs 2–3 weeks out drains recovery and confidence. Cap attempts at RPE 9.
- **Running the mock meet too close to the meet.** A full mock meet inside 2 weeks leaves too little time to recover.
- **Choosing the opener from hope.** Pick it from the peak's heavy singles and estimated max, not from the goal total.
- **Opening heavier than the heaviest training single.** The opener should be easier than anything hard in the peak.
- **Retesting a bad opener in the same week.** Lower it and move on.
- **Lenient judging and loose commands in training.** Red lights at the meet come from habits in training.
- **Not timing the warm-ups.** Lifters who don't know how long their warm-ups take start them too early or too late on meet day.
- **Ignoring the bench press in a novice's attempt plan.** Small plate jumps are a large percentage for a light bench; choose bench jumps carefully.

## Key takeaways

- A full mock meet (about 3–5 weeks out) rehearses the whole day; opener tests (about 7–14 days out) confirm the first attempts.
- Run mock meets with meet order, commands, strict judging, rehearsed warm-ups and meet timing; cap attempts at about RPE 9.
- In competition data, lifters opened at about 91–92% of their best successful lift; choose an opener the lifter could triple, at about RPE 7–8, lighter than the heaviest training single.
- Keep, raise, or lower the opener based on its RPE and technique in the test; don't retest a bad opener in the same week.
- Don't max in the last 2–3 weeks; heavy singles at RPE 8.5–9 give nearly the same information at much lower cost.
