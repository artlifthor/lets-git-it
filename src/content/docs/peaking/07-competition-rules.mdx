## In one minute

A lift only counts if two of three referees say it met the standard, so a lifter must be strong **and** legal. Every lift has commands: "Squat" and "Rack" for the squat; "Start", "Press" and "Rack" for the bench press; "Down" for the deadlift. Most red lights come from a short list: a high squat, moving before a command, an unlocked finish, or (in the bench) a bar that sinks or a body part that leaves the bench. This chapter summarises the IPF Technical Rulebook (2026 edition) as a coaching guide, including weigh-in, attempt and equipment rules, and gives a red-light checklist, drills and practice sessions for each lift. Rules change: always read the current rulebook and your meet's announcement before you compete.

## How to use this chapter

- The rules below are summarised from the **IPF Technical Rulebook, 2026 edition (effective 1 March 2026)**. They are facts, not evidence claims, so they carry no evidence labels; the coaching advice around them does.
- The IPF rules apply at all levels of IPF competition. In the Philippines, PAP is the IPF member, but local meets may announce their own details (for example equipment lists for novice divisions); read the meet announcement.
- The rulebook is the final word. Where this chapter simplifies, the rulebook wins. Several details changed in the 2026 edition, so older videos and notes may be out of date.

:::tip[In plain words]
This chapter explains the rules in coaching language. It is a guide, not the rulebook. Before every meet, read the current rulebook and the meet's own announcement, because rules do change.
:::

## Referees, lights and cards

- **Three referees:** the chief (centre) referee, who gives all commands, and two side referees.
- **Decision:** after the bar is back in the rack or on the platform, each referee shows a **white** (good lift) or **red** (no lift) light. Two or more whites = good lift.
- **Reasons:** after a red light, referees show a card, paddle or light colour for the reason:

| Card | Squat | Bench press | Deadlift |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Red** | Not deep enough | Bar not lowered to chest/abdomen, or touches the belt; elbows not lowered to shoulder level | Knees not locked; not standing erect with shoulders back |
| **2 · Blue** | Not upright with knees locked at start or finish; double bounce or downward movement on the way up | Downward movement of the whole bar during the press; elbows not locked at the start or finish | Downward movement of the bar before the finish; supporting the bar on the thighs (hitching) |
| **3 · Yellow** | Foot movement; missed command; spotter contact; elbows/upper arms supporting on the legs; dropping the bar; other rule breaks | Heaving or sinking the bar; missed command; upper-body thrust; feet, head, shoulders or buttocks moving; spotter contact; bar touching the uprights; other rule breaks | Lowering before "Down"; not controlling the bar down with both hands; foot movement; other rule breaks |

<Evidence level="practice" /> Coaches should learn this table: the colour tells you exactly what to fix for the next attempt.

:::tip[In plain words]
Three referees watch every lift. If at least two give a white light, the lift counts. If you get a red light, the colour of the card tells you why, so your coach knows what to fix before your next attempt.
:::

## The squat

**Commands:** "Squat" (with a downward arm movement) to start; "Rack" (backward arm movement) to finish.

**Execution, step by step**

1. Face the front of the platform. The bar sits horizontally across the shoulders; the 2026 rulebook sets a lowest legal bar position at the level of the rear deltoids (its change notes describe the aim as keeping the bar above the shoulder blades). Check the rulebook diagrams if you squat with a very low bar.
2. Hands, thumbs and fingers must be in full contact with the bar, anywhere inside or touching the inner collars. The thumb does not have to wrap around the bar.
3. Unrack (spotters may help lift the bar out), walk back, and stand **motionless, upright, knees locked**. Only then does the chief referee say "Squat". If you are not set within **5 seconds**, the referee will say "Replace" and explain why.
4. Descend until the **top surface of the legs at the hip joint is lower than the top of the knees**. Only one descent; the attempt begins when the knees unlock.
5. Stand up at will to upright with knees locked. No double bounce, no downward movement on the way up.
6. Hold still until "Rack", then walk the bar in. Foot movement after "Rack" is fine. You may not walk out through the front of the rack.

**Red-light checklist (squat)** <Evidence level="practice" />

- [ ] Waited for "Squat" with knees fully locked and body still
- [ ] Hip crease clearly below the top of the knee, judged from the side
- [ ] No dip, bounce or "hitch" on the way up
- [ ] Feet stayed planted (rocking heel to toe is allowed; stepping is not)
- [ ] Knees locked and body still before "Rack", and waited for it
- [ ] Elbows did not rest on the thighs
- [ ] Bar position legal; hands, thumbs and fingers in contact with the bar

**Drills** <Evidence level="practice" />

- **Command pause:** on every heavy single and top set, a training partner gives "Squat" after a random 1–3 second wait, and "Rack" only when the lifter is still.
- **Depth check:** film every top set from the side at hip height; learn what "clearly below" feels like, with a margin (squats right at parallel get mixed lights).
- **Tight walk-out:** two or three steps, the same every time, so the lifter is set within 5 seconds.

:::tip[In plain words]
Walk the bar out, stand still with straight knees, and wait for "Squat". Go down until your hip crease is below your knee, stand straight back up without dipping, and wait for "Rack". Squat a little deeper than you think you need to.
:::

## The bench press

**Commands:** "Start" (downward arm movement) when you are set with locked arms; "Press" (upward arm signal) when the bar is motionless on the chest; "Rack" (backward arm movement) at the finish.

**Execution, step by step**

1. Lie on the bench with **head, shoulders and buttocks** in contact with the bench (the rulebook pictures show the minimum acceptable contact). Hair must not hide the back of the head.
2. **Feet flat on the floor**, as flat as the shoe allows. Feet may move but must stay flat; they may not touch the bench or its supports, and you may not put your feet on the bench during set-up. Blocks or plates up to 30 cm can be used to build up the floor for shorter lifters.
3. **Thumbs-around grip** (no thumbless grip, no reverse grip). Hands no more than **81 cm apart**, measured between the forefingers; with the widest grip, the forefingers must cover the 81 cm marks.
4. Unrack yourself or take a hand-off from the spotters (a hand-off must bring the bar to arms' length). Hold the bar with **elbows locked** until "Start". Not set within 5 seconds? "Replace".
5. Lower the bar to the **chest or abdominal area** (not the belt). The **underside of both elbows must reach the level of, or below, the top of each shoulder joint**. Hold it **motionless** until "Press".
6. Press to straight arms, elbows locked. No downward movement of the whole bar during the press, no heaving or sinking, no body thrust, and no change in position (head, shoulders, buttocks, hands sliding).
7. Hold still at lockout until "Rack".

**Red-light checklist (bench press)** <Evidence level="practice" />

- [ ] Elbows locked and bar still before "Start"
- [ ] Bar touched chest or abdomen (not the belt); elbows down to shoulder level
- [ ] Bar visibly motionless before "Press", and waited for it
- [ ] No sink into the chest or bounce after "Press"; no extra arch or rib-cage drop to start the press
- [ ] Head, shoulders and buttocks stayed on the bench; feet flat and off the bench
- [ ] No downward movement of the whole bar during the press (one arm dipping slightly while the bar rises overall is judged by the referee; avoid it)
- [ ] Both elbows locked and bar still before "Rack", and waited for it
- [ ] Thumbs around the bar, grip inside the 81 cm marks

**Drills** <Evidence level="practice" />

- **Variable pause:** the partner calls "Press" after a random 1–3 second pause, so the lifter learns to stay tight and not anticipate.
- **Every rep paused in heavy work:** touch-and-go reps are fine for volume, but heavy sets and all singles use the full commands.
- **Feet-flat check:** if a lifter's heels lift under heavy load, set the feet where they stay flat (a slightly different position, blocks or different shoes).
- **Elbow-depth check:** film very high-arch or short-range lifters from the side to make sure the elbows reach shoulder level.

:::tip[In plain words]
Grip the bar with your thumbs around it, keep your head, shoulders, butt and flat feet in place, and lock your arms until you hear "Start". Lower the bar to your chest, keep it still until "Press", press it up, and hold it locked until "Rack". Never press before the command.
:::

## The deadlift

**Commands:** none to start. "Down" (downward arm movement) when you are finished and still.

**Execution, step by step**

1. Face the front, bar in front of the feet, any grip with both hands (double overhand, mixed or hook).
2. Lift in one movement until standing erect. **Any raising of the bar, or a deliberate attempt to raise it, counts as an attempt.**
3. Finish with **knees locked** and **shoulders back**: the 2026 rulebook defines this as the front of the shoulder (front deltoid) being behind the line of the bar.
4. No downward movement of the bar before the finish (a slight settle as the shoulders come back is not a fail). The bar may touch or slide up the thighs, but may not be **supported** on them (hitching).
5. Hold still until "Down", then lower the bar to the platform **under control with both hands**. Foot movement after "Down" is fine.

**Red-light checklist (deadlift)** <Evidence level="practice" />

- [ ] Knees fully locked at the top
- [ ] Standing erect with shoulders back (front deltoid behind the bar)
- [ ] No dip of the bar on the way up; no resting it on the thighs
- [ ] Feet stayed planted
- [ ] Held still and waited for "Down"
- [ ] Lowered with both hands on the bar (no dropping)

**Drills** <Evidence level="practice" />

- **Hold for "Down":** on all heavy singles, the partner says "Down" only after the lifter is still for about a second; train a 1–2 second hold.
- **Finish check:** film from the side; the lifter should finish tall with knees locked, without leaning back excessively.
- **Controlled lowering:** both hands stay on the bar to the floor every heavy rep.

:::tip[In plain words]
In the deadlift there is no start command: once you pull, it counts. Stand all the way up with straight knees and shoulders back, hold still until you hear "Down", and lower the bar with both hands. Don't rest the bar on your thighs on the way up.
:::

## Weigh-in and rack heights

- **When:** weigh-in starts no earlier than **2 hours** before the session and lasts **1.5 hours**. Lots are drawn; the lot number sets weigh-in order and breaks ties in lifting order.
- **How:** in a closed room with referees (and the lifter's coach or manager, of the same sex). Bring ID. Lifters may be weighed nude, or in minimal clothing (approved singlet, one approved T-shirt, standard underwear, no shoes).
- **Once only:** you are weighed once, unless you are outside your class limits; then you may return to the scale as often as time and the order allow, but only within the 1.5 hours.
- **First attempts:** the lifter or coach declares opening weights for **all three lifts** at weigh-in and signs the card.
- **Rack heights:** lifters must check squat rack and bench heights (and foot blocks) before the competition starts, and the lifter or coach **signs the rack height sheet**. Once the bar is announced as ready, the clock starts, and any rack changes come out of the lifter's one minute, unless the loaders set a nominated height wrongly.

<Evidence level="practice" /> Record your rack settings at your mock meet, and confirm them on the competition rack if you can.

:::tip[In plain words]
Weigh-in opens two hours before lifting and lasts an hour and a half. You hand in your three first attempts and your rack heights there. Check and sign your rack heights, because time spent fixing them later comes out of your one minute.
:::

## Attempt rules

**The basics**

- Three attempts per lift. The best good lift of each counts toward the total. Three misses on any lift = no total.
- Lifts go in **rounds**: everyone's first attempt, then everyone's second, then third.
- **Rising bar:** within a round, weights only go up. Lighter attempts lift first; equal weights go by lot number.
- The bar must be a multiple of **2.5 kg**, and each attempt must be at least **2.5 kg** heavier than the previous one. Record attempts are the exception: at least 0.5 kg above the current record, in the lifter's own championship.
- After a miss, you can repeat the same weight, but in the **next round** (you never follow yourself after a miss).

**Timing**

- **One minute to start** once the speaker calls you and the bar is ready. For squat and bench, "start" means the chief referee's start signal; for the deadlift, a determined attempt to lift the bar. Too late = "Rack", no lift.
- **One minute to hand in the next attempt**, counted from when the lights come on. If nothing is handed in: after a good lift, you get +2.5 kg; after a miss, the same weight.
- Leave the platform within **30 seconds** of finishing.
- In small flights (under 6 lifters), extra time is added at the end of each round.

**Changing attempts**

| Attempt | Can it be changed after it is handed in? |
|---|---|
| **First attempt (each lift)** | Once, up or down. Flight 1: up to 3 minutes before its first round starts. Later flights: up to three attempts before the end of the previous flight's last round of that lift |
| **Second attempt (all lifts)** | No |
| **Third attempt, squat and bench** | No |
| **Third attempt, deadlift** | Twice, up or down, as long as you have not already been called to the bar at the previous weight |

<Evidence level="practice" /> This is why the handler must have the next attempt decided before the lifter leaves the platform, and why deadlift thirds are where most last-minute tactics happen (see [Chapter 5: Attempt selection strategy](/lets-git-it/peaking/05-attempt-selection/)).

:::tip[In plain words]
You get one minute to start each lift and one minute after it to choose your next weight. Each attempt must go up by at least 2.5 kg. You can change your opener once, but second attempts and squat and bench thirds are fixed once handed in. Only the third deadlift can be changed, twice.
:::

## Equipment (classic)

All competition gear must meet the rulebook specifications; at IPF competitions, singlets, belts and knee sleeves (and all equipped gear) must come from manufacturers on the IPF **Approved List of Apparel and Equipment**. Equipment is inspected before the session (up to 30 minutes before the start); anything torn or dirty can be rejected, and wearing unchecked or illegal gear on the platform can lead to disqualification.

| Item | Key rules (summary) |
|---|---|
| **Singlet** | Approved, one-piece, form-fitting; legs 3–25 cm from the crotch. Long-legged singlets are allowed; knee sleeves go over, not under, them. The same singlet for all three lifts |
| **T-shirt** | Required under the singlet. No stretch/rubberised material, pockets or buttons; round neck; sleeves end below the deltoid and above the elbow and may not be rolled up |
| **Briefs, bra** | Standard commercial underwear; no supportive undergarments |
| **Socks** | Must not touch the knee sleeves; no full-length tights. **Shin-length socks are required for the deadlift** |
| **Belt** | Approved; max **10 cm** wide and **13 mm** thick; prong or lever buckle |
| **Shoes** | Indoor sports shoes, weightlifting/powerlifting shoes or deadlift slippers; sole max 5 cm; flat underside; laces or straps fastened |
| **Knee sleeves** | Approved; single-ply neoprene, max **7 mm** thick and **30 cm** long, centred on the knee, not touching socks or a standard singlet. Help putting them on is allowed |
| **Wrist wraps** | Max **1 m** long and **8 cm** wide; the thumb loop must be off the thumb during the lift; no wraps plus sweatbands together |
| **Head and tape** | No hats; hijab allowed. Two layers of tape on the thumbs; tape elsewhere only with official permission |
| **Substances** | Chalk, baby powder, talc and resin on body and clothing only (not wraps). No oil or grease; no adhesive under shoes (a spray of water is fine) |

Equipped lifting adds supportive suits, bench shirts and knee wraps, each with its own rules; this manual focuses on classic lifting.

<Evidence level="practice" /> Buy approved gear before the peaking block and train in it, so nothing on meet day is new. Bring spares (T-shirt, socks, wraps).

:::tip[In plain words]
Your singlet, belt and knee sleeves must be on the IPF approved list, and everything you wear has to fit the size rules. Wear a T-shirt under the singlet, and long socks for the deadlift. Get your gear early and train in it.
:::

## Conduct

- **Coaches:** only **one coach** may be at the platform, in the coaching area. At international events, coaches follow a dress code (team tracksuit or sport shorts with a team or IPF-approved T-shirt) and may not wear hats, take pictures or film from the coaching area. The number of coaches in the warm-up area is limited.
- **Lifters:** no wrapping, adjusting the costume or using ammonia in public view (adjusting the belt is allowed). Long hair must be tied back so it does not hide the face (squat, deadlift) or the back of the head (bench). Enter and leave the platform respectfully; throwing the belt or similar behaviour is not allowed, though reasonable celebration is fine.
- **Misconduct:** leads to an official warning, then disqualification; serious misconduct can mean immediate disqualification.
- **Appeals:** made to the Jury immediately after the event in question, by the coach or team manager (or the lifter if no coach is present). At some events the Jury may use instant replay.
- **Spotters:** they may help with the unrack and re-rack, but must not touch the bar or lifter between the commands unless the lift is in danger.
- **Anti-doping:** IPF meets are drug tested; see [Part 1, Chapter 1](/lets-git-it/foundations/01-what-is-powerlifting/).

:::tip[In plain words]
Behave well on and around the platform. Tie long hair back, don't wrap or change your gear in public, and don't throw your belt. Only one coach stands at the platform. If you think a decision was wrong, your coach must appeal to the Jury straight away.
:::

## Sample programs

Default lifter: squat 180 kg, bench 120 kg, deadlift 210 kg. Loads from the RPE chart in [Part 1, Chapter 5](/lets-git-it/foundations/05-measuring-intensity/). These templates add commands to normal training; they don't replace the program.

### Program 1: Commands in every heavy set (year-round)

A normal 4-day week, with the rules built in. Heavy squat (Monday) and heavy deadlift (Friday) are 4 days apart.

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load | Rule practice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 1, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~165 kg), then RPE 7 (~150 kg) | Partner gives "Squat" and "Rack" on the single and on the first rep of each set; side video for depth |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 4 × 3 | RPE 8 (~110 kg), then RPE 7 (~100 kg) | Full "Start", "Press", "Rack" on the single; every rep paused |
| Wed | Bench press | 4 × 5 | RPE 7 (~95 kg) | First rep of each set paused with commands; rest can be touch-and-go |
| Wed | Accessories | As programmed | — | — |
| Fri | Deadlift | 1 × 1, then 2 × 3 | RPE 8 (~192.5 kg), then RPE 7 (~175 kg) | Hold every rep's lockout; "Down" called on the single |
| Fri | Bench press (close grip) | 3 × 6 | RPE 7 (about 90 kg; close grip is usually a little weaker than competition grip) | Grip check: thumbs around |
| Sat | Squat (light) | 3 × 3 | ~75% (~135 kg) | Walk-out drill: set within 5 s, then wait for "Squat" |

**Why it's built this way.** Commands cost almost nothing to add, and they make the competition standard automatic. Using them on the heaviest work (where lifters most often rush) builds the habit where it matters.

### Program 2: Referee drill session (8–4 weeks out)

Once every 2–3 weeks, replace Monday's main work with this session. A training partner or coach sits in the chief referee's position and two others (or a phone on each side) act as side referees. Each lift is judged with the red-light checklists above, and every red light is explained with its card colour.

| Order | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load | Rules focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squat singles | 5 × 1 | RPE 7 (~160 kg) | Random 1–3 s wait for "Squat"; depth judged from the side; no "Rack" until still |
| 2 | Bench singles | 6 × 1 | RPE 7 (~107.5 kg) | Random 1–3 s pause before "Press"; feet flat; elbow depth; lockout before "Rack" |
| 3 | Bench "false command" drill | 3 × 1 | ~75% (~90 kg) | The referee sometimes delays or withholds "Press"; the lifter must keep the bar still |
| 4 | Back-off squat | 2 × 3 | RPE 7 (~150 kg) | Depth judged on every rep |

Deadlift singles with "Down" (4 × 1 at RPE 7, ~187.5 kg, with a 1–2 s hold) go on Friday, not on the same day as squats.

**Why it's built this way.** Singles at RPE 7 are heavy enough to expose rushed commands and borderline depth, but light enough to repeat several times. Getting judged by someone else (not the lifter's own eyes) is what makes it useful.

### Program 3: Rules rehearsal (2–3 weeks out)

Run inside the mock meet ([Chapter 3](/lets-git-it/peaking/03-mock-meets-openers/)). The goal is to rehearse the procedure, not to test maxes.

| Step | What to rehearse | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Weigh-in and cards | Lifter weighs in at a set time; writes three openers and rack heights on a card and signs it |
| 2 | Gear check | A teammate checks every item against the equipment table: approved list, sock length, sleeve position, T-shirt sleeves |
| 3 | Clock | "Bar is loaded" starts a 1-minute clock; the lifter must be under the bar and receive "Squat"/"Start" or start the deadlift in time |
| 4 | Attempts | After each attempt, the handler hands in the next weight in writing within 1 minute of the "lights" |
| 5 | Commands from a stranger | Someone the lifter rarely trains with gives commands, so the lifter reacts to the signal, not a familiar voice |
| 6 | Platform exit | Leave the platform within 30 s; belt off calmly |

Loads: openers and second attempts as planned for the meet (about RPE 7–8), per [Chapter 3](/lets-git-it/peaking/03-mock-meets-openers/).

**Why it's built this way.** Most procedure errors (late cards, missed clock, illegal socks) cost a whole attempt and have nothing to do with strength. Rehearsing them once, close to the meet, removes them.

## Common mistakes

- **Training without commands** and then rushing them at the meet: pressing before "Press", racking before "Rack", lowering before "Down".
- **Squatting to "about parallel".** Borderline depth gets mixed lights; train with a margin and check from the side.
- **Heels lifting or the body shifting in the bench** under heavy loads.
- **Ignoring rule changes.** The 2026 edition, for example, describes the bench elbow depth, the squat bar position and the deadlift shoulder position in new or clearer terms.
- **Unapproved or non-compliant gear** discovered at equipment check: short socks for the deadlift, a non-approved belt, rolled-up T-shirt sleeves.
- **Missing the 1-minute deadlines** for starting or for handing in the next attempt.
- **Not knowing the attempt-change rules,** for example trying to change a squat third attempt.

## Key takeaways

- A lift counts with two or more white lights; the card colour tells you the reason for a red light.
- Squat: wait for "Squat", hip crease below the top of the knee, no double bounce, wait for "Rack".
- Bench: thumbs-around grip, head–shoulders–buttocks on the bench, feet flat, elbows to shoulder level, bar still before "Press", lockout before "Rack".
- Deadlift: no start command; knees locked and shoulders back, no hitching, wait for "Down", lower with both hands.
- Weigh-in: up to 2 hours before, lasting 1.5 hours; openers and rack heights are handed in there.
- Attempts: at least +2.5 kg; one minute to start and one minute to hand in the next; only the opener (once) and the deadlift third (twice) can be changed.
- Train to the rules all year, and rehearse the procedure once before the meet.
