## In one minute

A peaking block is the last 3–6 weeks before a meet. It does four things at once: **intensity rises** to heavy singles, **volume falls** by roughly half, training **narrows to the competition lifts** done exactly as on the platform, and the lifter **rehearses openers** under meet conditions. Surveys of powerlifters show a consistent timeline: most volume about 5–8 weeks out, heaviest loads about 2–3 weeks out, final heavy squat and deadlift about 7–10 days out, final heavy bench press within a week, and the last session 2–4 days before the meet. RPE does the fine-tuning: heavy singles at RPE 8.5–9 tell you the lifter's current strength, and openers are chosen from those numbers, not from an old max. This chapter gives a full 5-week peak, a 4-week peak for a female lifter, and a 3-week peak for a lifter coming straight off a strength block.

## What a peaking block must do

A peak is the **realization** block of block periodization ([Part 2 · Chapter 6](/lets-git-it/building-programs/06-block-periodization/)). It does not build much new strength. It **reveals** the strength built in earlier blocks and makes it usable on the platform.

| Job | What changes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Raise intensity** | Sets of 3–5 → doubles and singles at 90%+ | Practise the heaviest loads you will lift at the meet |
| **2. Cut volume** | Sets fall about 40–60% by the final week | Clear fatigue so strength shows |
| **3. Increase specificity** | Competition lifts, commands, competition gear; accessories removed | Skill transfer; less fatigue from non-specific work |
| **4. Rehearse the meet** | Openers, commands, timing, warm-ups | Confidence; data for attempt selection |

<Evidence level="practice" /> Each block prepares the next one, and the peak is where that sequence pays off (phase potentiation; Israetel, Hoffmann & Smith). A peak cannot rescue a training cycle that never built strength.

:::tip[In plain words]
The last month before a meet is not for getting much stronger. It is for showing the strength you already built. You lift heavier, you do fewer sets, you only do the three competition lifts, and you practise exactly what you will do on the day.
:::

## Raising intensity

<Evidence level="practice" /> North American raw powerlifters reported their **highest training intensity about 2 weeks** before competition, with final heavy lifts at about **90–92.5% 1RM** (Travis et al., 2021b). Elite New Zealand lifters reported peak average intensity about 2 weeks out (Pritchard et al., 2016), and a systematic review of survey studies across the weightlifting sports found peak intensity typically **2–3 weeks out** (Winwood et al., 2026).

<Evidence level="practice" /> A typical progression of the **top set** across a 5-week peak:

| Weeks out | Top set | Approx. %1RM (RTS chart) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Double at RPE 8 | ~89% |
| 4 | Single at RPE 8 | ~92% |
| 3 | Single at RPE 8.5–9 | ~94–95.5% |
| 2 | Opener single at RPE 7–8 | ~89–92% |
| 1 (meet week) | Light singles and doubles | ~75–80%, no RPE target |

**Why not go to RPE 10 in training?** <Evidence level="practice" /> A true max costs more recovery and carries more risk than an RPE 9 single, and adds little information. In 15 powerlifters, even a true 1RM was rated only about 9.6–9.7 on average (Helms et al., 2017), so an RPE 9 single already sits close to a max. Save the true maxes for the platform.

:::tip[In plain words]
The weights get heavier until about two or three weeks before the meet. Your heaviest training lift should be a single that you could just about have done one more time. Leave the all-out lifts for competition day.
:::

## Cutting volume

<Evidence level="practice" /> Powerlifters typically reach their **highest training volume 5–8 weeks** before the meet (Travis et al., 2021b) and cut volume by roughly **41–60%** during the taper (Pritchard et al., 2016; Grgic & Mikulic, 2017; Travis et al., 2021b). This sits close to the 41–60% range that worked best in a meta-analysis of tapering in competitive athletes (Bosquet et al., 2007), though that evidence comes mostly from non-strength sports ([Chapter 1](/lets-git-it/peaking/01-peaking-and-tapering/)).

<Evidence level="practice" /> **What to cut, in order:**

1. **Accessories** — cut first; remove by about 2 weeks out (Pritchard et al., 2016).
2. **Variations** (pause squats, close-grip bench, deficit pulls) — reduce, then remove or keep one light set.
3. **Back-off sets** on the competition lifts — fewer, then none in the final week.
4. **Top sets** — keep them. They are the last thing to go.

**A typical volume pattern** (competition-lift sets per week, as % of the first peak week):

| Weeks out | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | 100% | ~80–85% | ~65–70% | ~50–60% | ~30–40%, all light |

:::tip[In plain words]
As the meet gets closer, do fewer sets, roughly half by the last two weeks. Drop the extra exercises first, then the lighter back-up sets. Keep the heavy sets until the very end.
:::

## Increasing specificity

- <Evidence level="practice" /> Elite lifters said their taper used the **same equipment and movements as competition**, removed assistance exercises, and was highly specific (Pritchard et al., 2016; Grgic & Mikulic, 2017).
- <Evidence level="practice" /> **Train to the rulebook.** Squat to competition depth and wait for the commands "Squat" and "Rack". Pause every bench press rep until a "Press" command; wait for "Rack". Lock out every deadlift and wait for "Down". Use the competition belt, sleeves, singlet and shoes. Practise your exact bench grip and foot position.
- <Evidence level="practice" /> **Keep the routine.** Same warm-up structure as on the day, same timing between heavy sets (about 3–5 minutes), same caffeine plan if the lifter uses caffeine. Nothing new in the last 3 weeks: no new shoes, belt, technique cue, or supplement.

**Is there another way to peak?** <Evidence level="emerging" /> In a 10-lifter pilot study before a national IPF-affiliated meet, a "daily max" approach (one single per lift at RPE 9–9.5 each session) raised some lifters' totals and lowered others', while lifters on a traditional periodized program with a taper improved or stayed the same (Androulakis-Korakakis et al., 2018). It is an option for some lifters, not a proven replacement for a structured peak.

:::tip[In plain words]
In the last weeks, every rep should look like a competition rep: full depth, paused benches, waiting for the referee's commands, wearing your meet gear. Don't try anything new close to a meet.
:::

## Timing the last heavy singles

Different lifts need different recovery times. Deadlifts need the most, bench press the least.

<Evidence level="practice" /> **What lifters report** (Travis et al., 2021b; Winwood et al., 2026):

| Lift | Last heavy session (> 85%) | Last session of any kind |
|---|---|---|
| Deadlift | 7–10 days out | About 6–8 days out |
| Squat | 7–10 days out | About 4–6.5 days out |
| Bench press | Under 7 days out | About 3–5 days out |
| All training | — | About 2–4 days out |

- <Evidence level="emerging" /> In 12 powerlifters, a 1-week step taper followed by 2 or 4 days without training maintained or improved maximal strength (Burke et al., 2023). <Evidence level="emerging" /> In 16 powerlifters, a 1-week step taper after an overreach week led to 1RM gains over time, with muscle measures that appeared to favour the step taper over a 3-week exponential taper (Travis et al., 2021c).
- <Evidence level="practice" /> Elite lifters said deadlifting takes longer to recover from than the other lifts (Pritchard et al., 2016). **Rule of thumb:** last heavy deadlift 9–10 days out, last heavy squat 7–12 days out, last heavy bench 5–7 days out.

:::tip[In plain words]
Do your last heavy deadlift about ten days before the meet, your last heavy squat about a week to twelve days before, and your last heavy bench about a week before. Your final training session should be two to four days before you compete.
:::

## Opener practice

<Evidence level="practice" /> The **opener** is the first attempt. It should be a weight the lifter can make even on a bad day, often described as a weight they could triple when fresh, about 90–92% of the expected best on the day. The peak gives one clean chance to **rehearse** it under meet conditions, usually 7–14 days out. How to choose and test openers, and when to run a full mock meet, is covered in [Chapter 3: Mock meets and testing openers](/lets-git-it/peaking/03-mock-meets-openers/).

**Choosing provisional openers from the heaviest singles.** Use the RTS chart in reverse ([Part 1 · Chapter 5](/lets-git-it/foundations/05-measuring-intensity/)): a 172.5 kg squat single at RPE 9 (95.5%) gives an estimated 1RM of 172.5 ÷ 0.955 ≈ 180.5 kg; at RPE 8.5 (93.9%), ≈ 183.5 kg. An opener near **RPE 7–7.5 on the day** (about 89–91% of that estimate) is about 162.5–167.5 kg.

:::tip[In plain words]
Your first attempt should be a weight you are sure of, even on a bad day. Use your heaviest training singles to estimate it, then practise it once, with commands, about one to two weeks before the meet.
:::

## How long should the peak be?

<Evidence level="practice" /> There is no research comparing peak lengths in powerlifters. These are coaching guidelines.

| Situation | Peak length |
|---|---|
| Intermediate lifter, A meet, coming from a general strength block | **4–5 weeks** (Program 1 or 2) |
| Coming straight off a strength block with sets of 1–5 at RPE 7–9, low fatigue | **3 weeks** (Program 3) |
| B or C meet you don't want to lose training time for | **1 week** mini-taper ([Chapter 1](/lets-git-it/peaking/01-peaking-and-tapering/), Program 3) |
| Advanced lifter, very high training volume, older, or high life stress | **5–6 weeks**, with a longer 2-week taper |
| Novice in a first meet | 2–3 weeks: a few heavy singles, opener practice, 1 easy week |

:::tip[In plain words]
Most intermediate lifters need about four to five weeks to peak. If you are already lifting heavy and feel fresh, three weeks can be enough. Beginners need less; very strong or older lifters may need a bit more.
:::

## Sample programs

RPE loads come from the RTS chart ([Part 1 · Chapter 5](/lets-git-it/foundations/05-measuring-intensity/)) and are rounded to 2.5 kg. **Loads in brackets are starting estimates; the RPE target decides.** If a lifter's heavy singles come in heavier than expected, everything after them, including openers, moves up. Meets are on a **Saturday**. No heavy squat and heavy deadlift sessions fall on consecutive days.

### Program 1: Five-week peak (intermediate man, 4 days per week)

**Lifter:** ~83 kg man; squat 180 kg, bench press 120 kg, deadlift 210 kg. He has finished an accumulation and a strength block. Goal for the meet: about 185 / 122.5 / 215 kg. **Training days:** Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday.

**Overview**

| Week | Weeks out | Focus | Squat / bench / deadlift sets | Accessories |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Last build week: heavy doubles plus volume | 8 / 16 / 4 (plus 3 light pause-squat sets) | 3 exercises |
| 2 | 4 | Heavier singles, volume down | 7 / 13 / 3 (plus 2 light) | 2 exercises |
| 3 | 3 | **Heaviest singles** (RPE 8.5–9) | 5 / 11 / 2 | 1 exercise |
| 4 | 2 | **Openers**, taper begins | 5 / 9 / 1 (mostly lighter) | None |
| 5 | Meet week | Light, sharp; last session 3 days out | 5 / 6 / 0–2 (all light) | None |

**Week 1 (5 weeks out)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 2, then 4 × 3 | RPE 8 (~89%, 160 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 150 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 2, then 4 × 4 | RPE 8 (~89%, 107.5 kg), then RPE 7 (~81%, 97.5 kg) |
| Mon | Chest-supported row | 3 × 10 | RPE 8 |
| Tue | Bench press | 4 × 5 | RPE 7 (~79%, 95 kg) |
| Tue | Pause squat (2 s), light | 3 × 3 | ~70% (125 kg), RPE ≤6 |
| Tue | Back extension | 3 × 12 | RPE 7 |
| Thu | Deadlift | 1 × 2, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~89%, 187.5 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 175 kg) |
| Thu | Bench press | 3 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Thu | Lat pulldown | 3 × 10 | RPE 8 |
| Sat | Squat | 3 × 2 | RPE 7.5 (~88%, 157.5 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~92%, 110 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |

**Week 2 (4 weeks out)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 1, then 3 × 2 | RPE 8 (~92%, 165 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 157.5 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~92%, 110 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Mon | Chest-supported row | 2 × 10 | RPE 8 |
| Tue | Bench press | 3 × 4 | RPE 7 (~81%, 97.5 kg) |
| Tue | Pause squat (2 s), light | 2 × 3 | ~70% (125 kg), RPE ≤6 |
| Thu | Deadlift | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8 (~92%, 192.5 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 185 kg) |
| Thu | Bench press | 3 × 2 | RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |
| Thu | Lat pulldown | 2 × 10 | RPE 8 |
| Sat | Squat | 3 × 2 | RPE 7.5 (~88%, 157.5 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5 (~94%, 112.5 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |

**Week 3 (3 weeks out): heaviest singles**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 170–172.5 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 157.5 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 2 × 3 | RPE 8 (~92%, 110 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Tue | Bench press | 3 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Tue | Chest-supported row | 2 × 10 | RPE 7 |
| Thu | Deadlift | 1 × 1, then 1 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 197.5–200 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 185 kg) |
| Thu | Bench press | 2 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Sat | Squat | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 155 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 112.5–115 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |

**After week 3: set provisional openers.** Estimate each 1RM from the heaviest single (load ÷ chart %), then pick an opener at about 89–91% of that estimate. Example: squat 172.5 kg at RPE 8.5 → e1RM ≈ 183.5 kg → opener **165 kg**; bench 115 kg at RPE 8.5 → ≈ 122.5 kg → opener **110 kg**; deadlift 200 kg at RPE 8.5 → ≈ 213 kg → opener **192.5 kg**.

**Week 4 (2 weeks out): openers**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon (12 days out) | Squat | Work up to 1 × 1 opener, then 2 × 2 | Opener 165 kg (target RPE 7–8), then RPE 7 (~86%, 155 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |
| Tue | Bench press, light | 3 × 3 | ~75% (90 kg), every rep paused, RPE ≤6 |
| Thu (9 days out) | Deadlift | Work up to 1 × 1 opener | Opener 192.5 kg (target RPE 7–8); no back-off sets |
| Thu | Bench press | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |
| Sat (7 days out) | Squat, light | 2 × 2 | ~80% (145 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | Work up to 1 × 1 opener, then 1 × 2 | Opener 110 kg with full commands, then RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |

**Week 5 (meet week)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon (5 days out) | Squat | 3 × 2 | ~75% (135 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 3 × 2 | ~80% (95 kg) |
| Mon | Deadlift (optional) | 2 × 1 | ~70% (147.5 kg), fast; skip if pulls recover slowly |
| Wed (3 days out) | Squat | 2 × 1 | ~75% (135 kg), with commands |
| Wed | Bench press | 3 × 1 | ~80% (95 kg), with commands |
| Thu–Fri | Rest | — | Weigh-in plan ([Chapter 4](/lets-git-it/peaking/04-making-weight/)), pack kit, sleep |
| Sat | **Meet** | — | Openers 165 / 110 / 192.5 kg (final check in Chapter 3) |

**Why it's built this way:**

- **Intensity rises** from doubles at RPE 8 (week 1) to singles at RPE 8.5–9 (week 3), then settles to opener singles at RPE 7–8 (week 4). The heaviest work is about 2–3 weeks out, as surveyed lifters report (Travis et al., 2021b; Winwood et al., 2026).
- **Volume falls** about 45% on bench press (16 → 9 sets) and about 40% on squat (8 → 5 sets) by week 4, and the deadlift falls to one opener single. Week 5 is all light.
- **Timing:** last heavy squat 12 days out, last heavy deadlift 9 days out, last heavy bench 7 days out, last session 3 days out; final sessions at 75–80% for squat and bench and 70% for deadlift, close to what North American lifters report (Travis et al., 2021b).
- **Spacing:** heavy deadlift (Thursday) and heavy squat (Saturday and Monday) are always 48 hours or more apart.

### Program 2: Four-week peak (female lifter, 63 kg class, 3 days per week)

**Lifter:** 25-year-old woman, 63 kg class; squat 120 kg, bench press 65 kg, deadlift 150 kg. Goal for the meet: about 125 / 67.5 / 155 kg. **Training days:** Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Bench press is her relatively weaker lift, so it is trained on every day.

**Week 1 (4 weeks out)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 2, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~89%, 107.5 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 2, then 4 × 4 | RPE 8 (~89%, 57.5 kg), then RPE 7 (~81%, 52.5 kg) |
| Mon | Chest-supported row | 3 × 10 | RPE 8 |
| Wed | Deadlift | 1 × 2, then 2 × 3 | RPE 8 (~89%, 135 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 125 kg) |
| Wed | Bench press | 4 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 55 kg) |
| Wed | Back extension | 2 × 12 | RPE 7 |
| Fri | Squat | 3 × 2 | RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |
| Fri | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~92%, 60 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 55 kg) |
| Fri | Dumbbell row | 2 × 10 | RPE 8 |

**Week 2 (3 weeks out): heaviest singles**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 112.5–115 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 3 × 2 | RPE 8 (~92%, 60 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 57.5 kg) |
| Wed | Deadlift | 1 × 1, then 1 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 140–142.5 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 132.5 kg) |
| Wed | Bench press | 3 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 55 kg) |
| Wed | Chest-supported row | 2 × 10 | RPE 7 |
| Fri | Squat | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |
| Fri | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 60–62.5 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 57.5 kg) |

**Provisional openers:** about 89–91% of the estimated 1RM from week 2. For example, if her week 2 singles were 115 kg, 62.5 kg and 142.5 kg, all at RPE 8.5, the estimates are about 122.5, 66.5 and 152 kg, giving openers of squat **110 kg**, bench press **60 kg**, deadlift **137.5 kg**. If her heaviest bench single was 60 kg at RPE 9 or harder, open at 57.5 kg instead.

**Week 3 (2 weeks out): openers**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon (12 days out) | Squat | Work up to 1 × 1 opener, then 2 × 2 | Opener 110 kg (target RPE 7–8), then RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 55 kg) |
| Wed (10 days out) | Deadlift | Work up to 1 × 1 opener | Opener 137.5 kg (target RPE 7–8); no back-off sets |
| Wed | Bench press, light | 3 × 3 | ~73% (47.5 kg), paused, RPE ≤6 |
| Fri (8 days out) | Squat, light | 2 × 2 | ~80% (95 kg) |
| Fri | Bench press | Work up to 1 × 1 opener, then 1 × 2 | Opener 60 kg with full commands, then RPE 7 (~86%, 55 kg) |

**Week 4 (meet week)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon (5 days out) | Squat | 3 × 2 | ~75% (90 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 3 × 2 | ~80% (52.5 kg) |
| Mon | Deadlift (optional) | 2 × 1 | ~70% (105 kg), fast |
| Wed (3 days out) | Squat | 2 × 1 | ~75% (90 kg), with commands |
| Wed | Bench press | 3 × 1 | ~80% (52.5 kg), with commands |
| Thu–Fri | Rest | — | — |
| Sat | **Meet** | — | Openers 110 / 60 / 137.5 kg |

**Why it's built this way:** with only 3 days a week, each session carries one heavy lower-body lift (squat Monday and Friday, deadlift Wednesday), always 48 hours apart, while bench press is practised three times a week. Weekly competition-lift sets fall from 7 / 13 / 3 (squat / bench / deadlift) in week 1 to 5 / 7 / 1 in week 3, with week 4 all light. Small plate jumps matter for her bench press: 2.5 kg is about 4% of her max, so openers and back-offs are chosen carefully. <Evidence level="practice" /> If she notices that certain days of her cycle reliably feel worse, keep the plan and let RPE adjust the loads on those days; on average, the menstrual cycle has a trivial or unclear effect on strength ([Part 3 · Chapter 10](/lets-git-it/advanced/10-special-populations/)).

### Program 3: Three-week peak straight off a strength block

**Lifter:** the same 180 / 120 / 210 kg man, finishing a 4-week strength block of sets of 2–5 at RPE 7–9. He has not deloaded but feels fresh. **Use this only if** the checks below pass.

| Check before starting | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Top sets at known loads still on target RPE (not rising for 2+ sessions) | Start week 1 | Do 3–4 lighter days first (half the sets, RPE ≤7), then start week 1 |
| No new or worsening joint pain | Start week 1 | Adjust exercises; see a health professional if pain is sharp or worsening |
| Lifter has done heavy singles (RPE 8+) in the last 3–4 weeks | Start week 1 | Use the 4–5 week peak instead |

**Week 1 (3 weeks out): heaviest singles**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Squat | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 170–172.5 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 157.5 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 3 × 3 | RPE 8 (~92%, 110 kg), then RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Tue | Bench press | 3 × 4 | RPE 7 (~81%, 97.5 kg) |
| Tue | Chest-supported row | 2 × 10 | RPE 7 |
| Thu | Deadlift | 1 × 1, then 1 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 197.5–200 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 185 kg) |
| Thu | Bench press | 2 × 3 | RPE 7 (~84%, 100 kg) |
| Sat | Squat | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 155 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | 1 × 1, then 2 × 2 | RPE 8.5–9 (~94–95.5%, 112.5–115 kg), then RPE 7.5 (~88%, 105 kg) |

**Week 2 (2 weeks out): openers**

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon (12 days out) | Squat | Work up to 1 × 1 opener, then 2 × 2 | Opener ~165 kg (target RPE 7–8), then RPE 7 (~86%, 155 kg) |
| Mon | Bench press | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |
| Tue | Bench press, light | 3 × 3 | ~75% (90 kg), paused, RPE ≤6 |
| Thu (9 days out) | Deadlift | Work up to 1 × 1 opener | Opener ~192.5 kg (target RPE 7–8) |
| Thu | Bench press | 2 × 2 | RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |
| Sat (7 days out) | Squat, light | 2 × 2 | ~80% (145 kg) |
| Sat | Bench press | Work up to 1 × 1 opener, then 1 × 2 | Opener ~110 kg with commands, then RPE 7 (~86%, 102.5 kg) |

**Week 3 (meet week):** the same as Program 1, week 5 (light squat and bench Monday, optional light deadlift, commands practice Wednesday, rest Thursday–Friday, meet Saturday).

**Why it's built this way:** the strength block already raised intensity and cut volume compared with accumulation, so the peak can start at the heaviest singles. Volume drops from the strength block's level by about half within two weeks. Because there is no gradual build, the readiness checks matter: starting a short peak while already fatigued is the most common way this goes wrong.

## Common mistakes

- **Peaking from an old max.** Loads based on a 1RM from months ago are wrong in both directions. Use RPE and the latest heavy singles.
- **Going to RPE 10 in training.** A training max costs recovery when it matters most. RPE 8.5–9 singles give almost the same information.
- **Keeping accessories and variations to the end.** They add fatigue without improving the meet result. Remove them by about 2 weeks out.
- **Heavy deadlifts in the final week.** The deadlift recovers slowest; finish heavy pulls 9–10 days out.
- **Cutting intensity along with volume.** The taper keeps heavy singles and doubles; only the number of sets falls.
- **Changing technique, gear or routine in the last 3 weeks.** Practise exactly what the lifter will do on the platform.
- **Not training to the rules.** High squats, touch-and-go bench presses and ignoring commands in training lead to red lights at the meet.
- **Following the plan blindly.** If the heavy singles say the lifter is stronger or weaker than expected, change the openers and the remaining loads.

## Key takeaways

- A peaking block lasts about 3–6 weeks and raises intensity, cuts volume, narrows to the competition lifts, and rehearses the meet.
- Powerlifters report their highest volume about 5–8 weeks out and highest intensity about 2–3 weeks out, with final heavy lifts at about 90–92.5% 1RM.
- Cut volume by about half by the final two weeks: accessories first, then variations, then back-off sets; keep the top sets.
- Last heavy deadlift about 9–10 days out, squat about 7–12 days out, bench press about 5–7 days out; last session 2–4 days out.
- Use RPE 8.5–9 singles to estimate current strength and choose openers at about 89–91% of that estimate; rehearse them 7–14 days out.
- Choose the peak length by the lifter's level, fatigue and the importance of the meet; most intermediates need 4–5 weeks.
